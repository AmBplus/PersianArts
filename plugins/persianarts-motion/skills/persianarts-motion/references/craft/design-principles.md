# Design principles for motion frames (short)

- **One hero per frame.** Size, contrast, position and motion all point to it. Everything else is quieter.
- **Hierarchy in three levels max**: hero, support, detail. A fourth level is noise.
- **Type scale from the theme**, never ad-hoc sizes. Persian needs ~5–10 % more size than Latin for the same
  presence; display weights one step heavier.
- **Space is a material.** Margins ≥ 6 % of the short side; let a hero sit in emptiness. Dense frames need a grid.
- **Composition variety**: alternate centred / thirds / split / full-bleed across scenes; RTL reading enters from
  the right, so the right half is the "start" of a frame.
- **Colour discipline**: neutral base, one accent with a job (the thing to look at), a second accent only for a
  second meaning (e.g. before/after). No decorative gradients without a subject reason.
- **Contrast** ink/background ≥ 4.5 for body text, ≥ 3 for large display text (≥ 48 px at 1080p); text over imagery or motion needs a scrim or a quiet zone.
- **Information density follows the dial**: at density ≤ 4 one idea per frame; build complex diagrams in steps,
  each step on a beat, and hold the completed state.
- **Numbers deserve moments**: a key number gets its own beat, large, with a count or a land, and Persian digits.
- **Safe areas**: 9:16 keeps text out of the top 12 % and bottom 20 % (platform UI); 16:9 title-safe 90 %.
- **Consistency beats novelty**: a small system (3 curves × 3 durations, one grid, one type scale) used everywhere
  reads as designed; a new trick per scene reads as a reel.
